# 4. Crypto Self-Custody Education

There is definitely a legitimate educational business here.

I would **not** start by holding people's cryptocurrency, managing their portfolios, exchanging currencies for them, or operating your own remittance service.

Philippine regulation is substantially more serious once you're exchanging, transferring, safekeeping or operating crypto services. The SEC adopted Crypto-Asset Service Provider rules in 2025, and BSP continues to regulate VASPs; a 2026 BSP memo specifically requires financial institutions to deal only with authorized VASPs/CASPs. ([SEC Philippines][5])

BSP also continued its moratorium on issuance of new VASP licenses beginning September 1, 2025. ([Bureau of the Treasury][6])

So:

**Education = interesting.**

**Custody/exchange/remittance = regulatory business.**

### Better remittance opportunity

Build a **remittance optimization service/software layer** that compares legitimate rails rather than becoming the financial intermediary yourself.

For example:

> “You want to send $500 from the U.S. to the Philippines. Here are today's legitimate options, their fees, FX spread and arrival time.”

Then partner with licensed providers.

## One legal/business issue to keep in mind

Your citizenship/business structure matters. If you're operating as a **foreign national rather than a Philippine citizen**, Philippine foreign-investment rules can materially affect smaller domestic-market businesses.

The BOI's current guidance under RA 11647 generally references **US$200,000 paid-in capital** for certain 100%-foreign-owned domestic-market enterprises, with potential reduction to **US$100,000** for qualifying advanced-technology/startup businesses or businesses employing at least 15 direct Filipino employees. Restricted sectors require separate analysis. ([Bangko ng Pilipinas][13])

So before putting serious money into a Philippine corporation, we should determine exactly what citizenship/status and ownership structure you're working with.
