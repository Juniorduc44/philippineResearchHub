## One-liner

A shared, fee-per-crate cold room at the edge of a producing wet market so harvest is cooled (and if needed frozen) instead of dumped at the bagsakan.

## Problem

- Who: smallholders, fishers, and aggregators isolated from demand
- Where: a producing municipality with a bagsakan / wet market (LGU not yet filed)
- How the pain shows up: dump-selling, daily spoilage, farmers earning little or nothing, consumers paying more for less-fresh food
- Source: `research/library/2026-09-20-food-prices-cold-chain-transcript.md` (Glacier / Breaking Ground interview — speaker claims, not PSA)

## Customer

- Segment: growers and barangay assemblers who already bring volume to one market
- Region / city: pick one producing LGU; do not file as "nationwide"
- Ability to pay: unknown until a specific crop and market is chosen

## Offer

- What they get: crate or pallet space at a stated temperature, receiving log; freeze only if the crop and power plan support it
- Price band (PHP, year): unknown

## Why Philippines

- Archipelago + farm-to-market delay: quality preservation (cold storage) is what the interviewee says is missing even where the nautical highway exists.
- Rooms are needed **near the farm** and **near demand centers** (he names Manila, Cebu, Davao). A community room is the farm-side end, not a 12,000-pallet plant.
- Supply and demand do not happen at the same time; cooling is the pause so growers are not forced to dump.

## Model

- Revenue: daily or weekly storage fee per crate; optional hauling
- Repeat vs one-time: repeat, harvest-calendar driven; peak-season shortage is when the speaker says the capacity gap is felt
- Key cost: power, refrigerant maintenance, **backup generation**, night security

## Location

- Region / city: not filed
- Site type: edge of a wet market or packing house with reliable power **and** a generator plan

## Operations

- People: receiving clerk + night guard + technician on call
- Supply: empty crates, diesel or grid
- Hours / seasonality: harvest peaks; typhoon calendar
- KPI from the transcript (scale-invariant): the room must stay cold; an outage spoils the stock

## Competition

- Named PH players (interview only): Glacier Cold Chain (large plants, not barangay rooms); DA-built rooms that can decay into dry warehouses if not privately operated
- Gap: farm-adjacent crate-scale cooling vs city-scale pallets — **unverified at a chosen LGU**

## Risks

- Regulatory: food handling and electricity
- Supply / disaster: typhoon, brownouts (transcript: thin power reserves)
- Collection / demand: growers may still dump-sell if the fee is high vs farm-gate
- Governance: government-operated rooms in the interview failed after startup (Benguet example — speaker’s account)
