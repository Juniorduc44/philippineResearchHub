# HeliaSol façade film (lightweight OPV)

## One-liner

Sell and install Heliatek HeliaSol adhesive organic-PV film on Philippine buildings that cannot take conventional rooftop silicon — especially warehouse and factory façades.

## Problem

- Who: owners of light metal warehouses, factories, sheds, malls, schools, hangars whose roofs cannot carry racked modules, or who have large unused walls
- Where: first investigation is industrial/warehouse stock nationwide; no LGU picked
- How the pain shows up: conventional PV is rejected on weight, penetrations, or wind racking; façades generate 0 kWh today

## Customer

- Segment: B2B building owners and facility managers; not DIY homeowners (Heliatek requires authorized installers)
- Region / city: unknown — start with one warehouse wall after a quote, not a house
- Ability to pay: unknown until landed ₱/W

## Offer

- What they get: HeliaSol 436-2000 OPV film (~436 × 2000 mm, ~0.87 m², 1.6 kg, 50–55 W, 7.2–8.0% aperture efficiency per manufacturer datasheet) glued to an approved substrate
- Price band (PHP, year): **unknown**. No PH distributor invoice on disk

## Why Philippines

- Many light steel buildings; roofs that fail a conventional statics check; expensive grid power (rate not cited — PNA article Cloudflare-blocked)
- Hot metal skins: datasheet states +0.00 %/°C from 25°C to 65°C (manufacturer, not a Cebu field log)
- DOE DC2026-08-0017 (20 Aug 2026) eases **own-use zero-export and ≤1 kW micro-solar** — a façade string may still be a larger SGF / net-metering case

## Model

- Revenue: project install (₱/W or ₱/m²) plus possible O&M / inspection of edges
- Repeat vs one-time: one-time capex per wall; repeat if you inspect/replace films
- Key cost: film + freight + duties + primers + certified labor + inverter/BOS + substrate repair

## Capital

- Band (PHP): unknown
- Main uses of funds: 10–20 film torture-test kit, instruments, primers, inverter, site prep — **not** a container
- Estimate year: 2026
- Source: none (no quote)

## Licenses

- Entity (DTI / SEC / CDA): sole prop DTI BNRS if Filipino owner; distribution partnership may want a corp — `research/library/2026-09-20-dti-start-grow.md`
- BIR: TIN + ATP unknown fees
- LGU: mayor’s permit at the install city; building/electrical as LGU requires
- Sector: ERC / DU interconnection. MSS (≤1 kW plug-and-play) under DC2026-08-0017 is **not** assumed for a multi-kW wall. PEC/electrical permit unknown
- Source: DOE media release `research/library/2026-09-21-doe-own-use-solar.md`

## Location

- Region / city: not filed
- Site type: light metal warehouse or factory **façade** with ≥1° drain slope, solid substrate, not coastal edge-zone until wind engineering exists

## Operations

- People: authorized Heliatek installer (manufacturer), not a weekend crew
- Supply: films, primers (Triflex mentioned in user guide for bitumen), spare films, adhesion/edge inspection
- Hours / seasonality: install weather-limited (user guide: dry substrate, >8°C, no rain/strong wind)

## Competition

- Named PH players: conventional rooftop EPC (Jinko-class silicon) — **do not compete ₱/W on a strong roof**
- Gap: buildings other contractors refuse for weight/penetration. Heliatek PH distributor: **unknown**

## Risks

- Regulatory: ERC/DU class of the system; professional-install-only product
- Supply / disaster: substrate/paint/corrosion failure (adhesive can hold paint that leaves the steel); typhoon suction at edges/corners — ±1600 Pa design load is **not** a km/h typhoon rating; no PH field history on disk
- Collection / demand: ~16 m²/kWp (operator arithmetic from 55 W / 0.87 m²); vertical façade yields unknown vs tilted roof
