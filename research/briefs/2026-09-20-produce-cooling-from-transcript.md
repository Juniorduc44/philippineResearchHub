# Brief: Produce cooling / freezing (from the food-prices transcript)

- Date: 2026-09-20
- Status: draft
- Source: `research/library/2026-09-20-food-prices-cold-chain-transcript.md`

## Question
What in this interview actually argues for **cooling or freezing produce**, not just “food is expensive”?

## What the speaker said
1. **Dump, then spoil.** Isolated bagsakan (Bankerohan named) is where perishable harvest is left because there is no timed path to a buyer. Daily spoilage gets worse; middlemen buy cheap; consumers pay more for less-fresh food.
2. **Time gap.** Supply and demand are not simultaneous. Cold storage is the pause that lets a farmer *not* sell in desperation, add value (sort, process, freeze), and hit a better price.
3. **Where the room should sit.** Not only city warehouses. **Near the farm** and **near Cebu / Manila / Davao**. That is the same geometry as our community-cold-storage stub (wet-market edge), at a much smaller scale than Glacier’s 12,000-pallet plants.
4. **Blast freeze** is named as the step between storage and port/export — relevant if an LGU later wants processing beside the room, not for a one-crate MVP.
5. **Power is the kill switch.** Refrigeration failure spoils the stock. Any community room needs a generator plan; the transcript treats backup power as non-optional.
6. **Who should run it.** He argues DA-built rooms that government kept operating decayed into dry warehouses (Benguet). Private operation after public build is his prescription — useful as a *hypothesis* for CDA/coop vs LGU-run, not as a law.

## What this is not
- Not a capital quote, kWh rate, or Cebu LGU permit.
- Not proof that a barangay crate-room earns money.
- Pallet-capacity numbers are the interviewee’s, not PSA.

## Implication for `community-cold-storage`
The **problem statement is no longer a format example**. The gap is still: pick one crop and one LGU, then price power and the room. Do not jump to Glacier-scale pallets.
