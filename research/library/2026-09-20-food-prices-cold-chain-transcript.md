## Claims that bear on cooling / freezing / produce storage
- Harvest is dumped at isolated *bagsakan* (e.g. Bankerohan, Mindanao) because farmers, fishers, and aggregators are disconnected from demand. Spoilage is the easy path.
- Wastage is passed to consumers as higher prices and lower freshness.
- Cold-chain **deficit along farm-to-market roads** spoils yield; farmers sell out of desperation because supply and demand do not happen at the same time.
- Speaker: theoretically the country needs ~**1.2 million pallets** of cold storage for agriculture, restaurants, catering, post-consumption; Glacier ~12 plants / ~80,000 pallets / ~15% of that (his figures).
- Shortage is felt hardest in **peak consumption**; off-peak (Jan–Sep) the gap is smaller.
- Missing piece vs the nautical highway: **quality preservation** — cold storage **near farm sites and near demand centers** (Manila, Cebu, Davao).
- Process he names: cold storage, **blast freezing**, port terminal, then demand centers.
- Ube (and similar) cannot scale to export without a mature corridor; processing can sit **beside** a cold plant.
- Government-built small rooms (example: **Benguet**) worked then became ordinary warehouses because operation stayed with government, not a private operator.
- His model: public finance / subsidy, **private operate ~25 years**; machines need engineers, 100% availability, backup power. A GMAC-scale outage is “billions of pesos” / ~12 million kilos at that site.
- Thin national power reserves → selective outages; backup generation is part of the business model.
